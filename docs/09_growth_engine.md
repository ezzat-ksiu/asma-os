# FOLIAVERE — Organic Growth Engine, SEO Library and Gulf B2B Playbook

ملخص تنفيذي 1: يركز النمو الأولي على إثبات العرض وبيع أول مجموعة من الإصدارات دون إعلانات مدفوعة.
ملخص تنفيذي 2: يستهدف التواصل البالغين والجهات المؤسسية فقط، ولا يجمع بيانات الأطفال أو ينشئ ملفات تعريف للآباء.
ملخص تنفيذي 3: يتضمن الملف ثلاثين عنواناً تحريرياً محدداً بع intent بحثي، لا صفحات آلية مكررة.
ملخص تنفيذي 4: تعتمد المقاطع الاجتماعية على رسوم وكتب نموذجية اصطناعية مع موافقة المؤسس قبل النشر.
ملخص تنفيذي 5: يتم اقتحام الفنادق والمدارس بتجربة مدفوعة من خمس نسخ، لا بهدايا مجانية عشوائية أو ادعاءات تحويل مختلقة.

## 1. Growth thesis

FOLIAVERE is not ready to scale acquisition by volume. It must first make the value difference visible: an editorially directed commission, proper-name accuracy, multilingual layout, a real physical proof, a controlled production record and privacy-minimised intake. Growth is an evidence loop: show one credible sample, invite a qualified adult buyer or institution, close a paid commission, deliver correctly, then use the resulting approved learning to improve the offer.

**Non-negotiable growth rules**

- No paid advertisements until cumulative verified net profit reaches USD 10,000 and a founder signs the exact campaign and spend cap.
- No real child likeness, customer photograph, private manuscript or customer story in public marketing.
- No scraping parents, child-interest groups or personal family data. The outbound agent researches public organisations and adult enquiries only.
- No fake reviews, fabricated hotel logos, invented case studies, false “first” claims or unverified delivery claims.
- No bulk SEO page generation. Google classifies mass unoriginal pages intended to manipulate rankings as scaled content abuse regardless of whether AI created them.
- No free physical samples to dozens of executives. Use a paid micro-pilot with a controlled scope.

## 2. First 100 customers — zero ad spend operating sequence

These are acquisition steps and evidence gates, not calendar promises. Reaching 100 customers is an outcome target, not a guaranteed result.

### Gate A — A credible offer

1. Select one live SKU and one target destination. Do not sell an SKU whose print specification, shipping route and quote are unverified.
2. Finish the six synthetic sample books and disclose them as portfolio demonstrations.
3. Produce a physical sample of the exact 32-page product and photograph it honestly. Label digital renders as mockups.
4. Publish one tier comparison with the exact page count, paper/binding options, proof rounds, exclusions and refund/correction policy.
5. Record the unit economics using current supplier quote, shipping quote, provider fee and 2% minimum reprint reserve.
6. Test the full intake-to-proof journey with test data before accepting personal data.

**Exit evidence:** one physically inspected sample, one live destination quote, one cost sheet, one complete customer proof and a public product page that matches the actual SKU.

### Gate B — Buyer discovery without private-data harvesting

1. Interview adult purchasers who voluntarily agree to discuss premium family books, memoirs or gifts.
2. Ask what they last bought, why they bought it, what they disliked, how they chose the recipient and what would make them trust a commissioned book.
3. Do not ask a child to participate in sales research; do not collect children's photos, names, school, location or behaviour.
4. Record only the minimum adult feedback needed for product decisions, with consent and a published research notice.
5. Separate “that sounds lovely” from a real purchase signal. A paid deposit or completed paid order is stronger evidence than compliments.

**Exit evidence:** objection register, buyer language, accepted price range, at least one completed paid order or a clear no-demand finding.

### Gate C — Direct organic distribution

1. Publish the sample collection and making-of documentation on the official site.
2. Use founder-owned social accounts to publish synthetic book art, typography tests, physical-paper comparisons and page-to-print demonstrations.
3. Send personal, relevant proposals only to public business contacts with a lawful contact route; identify the sender and make opt-out simple.
4. Participate in adult book, design, family-history and hospitality communities only where self-promotion is permitted. Answer questions usefully before sharing a commercial link.
5. Ask satisfied adult customers for an optional review. Publication permission is separate from survey participation.
6. Offer an optional referral invitation after confirmed delivery. Never condition support or product quality on referral.
7. Keep a small pipeline: `qualified → discovery → proposal → paid → delivered → feedback`. Remove unresponsive or opted-out records.

**Exit evidence:** paid orders with contribution statements, source-of-lead attribution and recorded customer feedback—not follower count alone.

### Gate D — B2B paid micro-pilot

1. Select one hotel property or one school operator; do not pitch an entire global group before proving one use case.
2. Identify a public, role-relevant contact: hotel general manager, guest-experience lead, brand/marketing lead or procurement; school owner/operator, principal, library or communications lead.
3. Prepare a fictional sample tied to the organisation's broad guest or learning context without using its logo, property photography or student details unless licensed and approved.
4. Offer a paid five-copy pilot. The standard hospitality pilot planning price is USD 1,500 before exceptional travel, tax, special packaging or third-party licensed material.
5. Set the content scope, delivery location, rights, proof rounds, physical finish and acceptance criteria in a short proposal.
6. Deliver and measure editorial approval, print quality, delivery reliability, recipient feedback and commercial willingness to repeat.

**Exit evidence:** signed commercial order through an authorised adult/legal contracting party, captured payment, print/delivery proof and an outcome report.

### Gate E — Refine and repeat

1. Identify the highest-contribution product and the most reliable supplier route.
2. Fix the top three recurring customer objections and top three production defects.
3. Make the sample portfolio match the real finished product.
4. Improve the checkout and product page where buyer questions occur.
5. Build a case study only from a client-approved, factual result with written publication permission.
6. Repeat the best acquisition path until the order economics and founder workload are stable.

**First-100 acquisition mix to test (planning target, not forecast):** 40 adult consumer referrals and repeat introductions, 30 direct organic enquiries from owned content/search, 20 community and partner introductions, and 10 B2B pilot-derived buyers or recipients who independently commission an edition. These categories must be de-duplicated by actual paying buyer; a B2B contract is not counted as five separate B2C customers unless five independent buyers paid.

## 5. Gulf B2B playbook

### 5.1 Who to contact

**Luxury hospitality:** general manager, director of marketing, guest experience director, brand director, head of procurement, corporate communications. Start with a single property or a specific guest programme. Do not pitch the corporate group as if a property-level contact can approve a group-wide contract.

**International and private schools:** school owner/operator, principal, head of communications, library director, innovation/enrichment lead or procurement. Student-identifiable stories are outside the initial pilot; the pilot uses fictional characters and school-approved curricular themes.

### 5.2 What to send

1. A short note that names the recipient organisation and a specific public, relevant use case.
2. One synthetic sample book and a one-page production specification.
3. A clear statement that the sample is fictional and does not imply an existing partnership.
4. A paid pilot scope covering five copies, language, delivery destination, one proof cycle, licensing, quote validity and evaluation metrics.
5. A privacy and rights appendix with data categories, approved assets, retention and publication controls.
6. A simple acceptance sheet: correct names/text, physical print quality, delivery integrity and buyer feedback.

### 5.3 Paid five-book micro-pilot

**Planning price:** USD 1,500 for one tightly scoped five-copy pilot before tax and exceptional items. This price is a test hypothesis, not market evidence. The price must be recalculated against supplier quotes, destination shipping, content rights, editing effort and founder workload.

| Component | Included in base pilot | Excluded unless separately quoted |
|---|---|---|
| Copies | Five copies of one approved title | Additional language versions or more than five copies |
| Editorial | One defined story / brand brief and one proof cycle | Interviews, extensive research or manuscript beyond agreed scope |
| Artwork | Synthetic or client-licensed approved assets | Unlicensed stock, photography or commissioned external illustrator |
| Production | One printer SKU with live quote | Foil, embossing, rigid gift box or special freight |
| Data | Organisation-approved non-sensitive material | Student/guest personal data or child photographs |
| Measurement | Print QA, delivery check and a short feedback summary | Guaranteed NPS or guaranteed repeat contract |

### 5.4 First ten institutional clients — acquisition design

- Build a verified prospect list from public directories, not scraped private contacts.
- Segment five hospitality accounts and five education accounts; do not assume they share the same buying process.
- For every account, record the public source, target use case, adult decision role, procurement route and opt-out state.
- Offer the same paid five-copy pilot structure while adapting the story concept to the institution's approved material.
- Require a paid order before production work begins, with a signed scope from a person legally authorised to contract.
- Do not send unsolicited physical gifts to senior executives. A low-cost digital sample and a paid pilot request are more measurable and create less waste.
- After each pilot, record proof accuracy, defects, on-time delivery, recipient feedback, contribution margin and founder minutes.
- Expand within an account only after a repeat order or a written referral with permission.

**Conversion tracking:** count qualified account → discovery meeting → paid pilot proposal → paid pilot → delivered pilot → repeat order. Do not use a speculative “20–30% conversion” rate.

## 7. Platform publishing constraints

- TikTok Direct Post requires the app's Content Posting API configuration, a `video.publish` authorisation, user consent and a verified media URL/domain where URL pull is used. TikTok states that unaudited clients are restricted to private viewing until the client passes its audit. Do not promise public automated publishing before approval.
- LinkedIn Posts API requires an access token with the appropriate member or organisation social permission and an authorised account role; versioned API headers must be maintained.
- Meta publishing also requires an eligible professional account, app permissions and account-holder authorisation. Platform access is not equivalent to a free, unrestricted scheduler.
- A self-hosted scheduler requires a compute host and supported APIs. It is not automatically zero-cost. Keep it off until the platform integration and hosting budget are approved.
