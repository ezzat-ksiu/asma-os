## 1. Evidence standard

This document uses named, published behavioural research. It does not claim that any study proves a particular conversion rate for FOLIAVERE. Research findings are directional; transfer to a high-priced personalised publishing offer must be tested with real buyers. The five personas below are operational hypotheses, not demographic facts or research-derived market segments.

## 2. Luxury perception: price anchoring, scarcity and costly signals

**Price anchoring** means that an initial reference price can influence how subsequent prices are judged. A premium tier at $649 will not “feel cheap” by magic. It must be framed against a transparent scope: editorial development, multiple illustrations, proof stages, physical quality, named edition record, delivery and support. A false reference price or inflated “was” price is deceptive and must not be used.

**Signalling theory / costly signals** helps explain why visible craft can signal care and quality when it is difficult to fake: paper feel, binding integrity, typography, accurate print registration and restrained finishing. A gold graphic by itself is a cheap visual signal; a consistent physical object that withstands handling is stronger evidence. Use supplier samples and independently documented quality checks instead of claims like “Hermès quality”.

**Scarcity and uniqueness:** a serial number is a verifiable edition identifier. It does not, by itself, prove that no comparable template or generic composition was ever used. The customer promise should be narrowly defined: “This commissioned copy is produced for this customer under this order and is not resold as the same personalised edition.” State what may be reused (generic layouts, fonts, illustration techniques and non-identifying components).

Commercial implication: show the exact product, number of pages, revision scope, cover, paper and delivery estimate before purchase. The customer is paying for curated authorship and physical quality, not a vague promise of AI magic.

## 3. Gift-giving psychology: parents and grandparents

Gino and Flynn's 2011 Journal of Experimental Social Psychology paper, “Give them what they want: The benefits of explicitness in gift exchange”, reports across five studies that recipients appreciate explicitly requested gifts more than givers expect; givers can overestimate the value of an unsolicited “thoughtful surprise”. [Stanford publication record](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/give-them-what-they-want-benefits-explicitness-gift-exchange).

**Application:** the buyer should be allowed to choose the recipient's interests, language, reading comfort, preferred values and whether the story should be playful or calm. Offer a “gift preference note” that the buyer can complete with facts already known, rather than forcing an elaborate surprise that might misrepresent the recipient.

**Parents:** the main job is not “make my child the hero at any price”. It is to create a shared reading ritual with accurate names, safe themes, age-appropriate language and no public use of the child's image. Avoid suggesting the book will fix behaviour, intelligence or emotional difficulties.

**Grandparents:** the job is a meaningful bridge across generations. Include a family-approved phrase, a memory, or a dedication. Avoid guilt-based copy such as “before it's too late” or fear of mortality.

**Experiential gifts:** Chan and Mogilner's work on experiential gifts and relationships (Journal of Consumer Research, 2017) examines how experiences can strengthen relationships. A personalised book can be presented as a shared activity—choosing the story, reading together, preserving a memory—rather than only as an object. [DOI: 10.1093/jcr/ucw067](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucw067).

## 4. Legacy psychology: generativity, continuity and nostalgia

**Generativity** (Erikson) refers broadly to contributing to and guiding later generations. A family legacy book can provide a structured way to record values, places, recipes, language and stories. It must not imply that the book preserves a person completely or substitutes for a relationship.

**Self-continuity and nostalgia:** published work reviews nostalgia as a psychological resource that can support social connectedness and meaning in some circumstances. [Sedikides et al., 2008 review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-8721.2008.00595.x); [Routledge et al., 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/gpr0000109). Translate this into design through concrete memory prompts: “What did the kitchen smell like?”, “Which saying did your family repeat?”, “What would you like a younger relative to understand?”

**Mortality salience:** do not use mortality reminders as a conversion tactic. Even for legacy products, avoid urgency that exploits illness, bereavement or family anxiety. Offer calm prompts and clear ownership/export options.

## 5. Personalisation and the name effect

Kou, Shuai and Powpaka's 2021 article, “The name effect in customization service: the role of psychological ownership and self-threat”, reports an experiment where placing a customer's name on a product positively affected product attitude, mediated by psychological ownership; it also reports moderation by self-threat. [Journal of Service Theory and Practice](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2055622521000016), DOI 10.1108/JSTP-08-2019-0181.

**Application:** names must be exact, correctly transliterated and checked in context. A wrong name is not a cosmetic defect; it attacks the very mechanism that makes personalisation meaningful. Create a pre-print name proof with the name highlighted and require explicit approval. Never infer a child's identity, culture, religion, ethnicity or family structure from a photo.

## 6. Unboxing: tactile coherence, not “dopamine engineering”

A 2025 *Scientific Reports* study explored multisensory packaging and reported effects of package interaction and texture on perceived attractiveness and emotional judgements. It used only 36 participants and self-report measures; the authors note limits and do not establish a physiological dopamine effect. [Xiao et al., 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-24077-6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41203669/).

**Application:** make the opening sequence simple and legible: outer mailer, protective wrap, book, certificate, personal letter, and one QR. Avoid perfume or scent as default; scent can be polarising and may trigger sensitivity. Do not create excessive packaging waste to manufacture a video moment. Use a satisfying reveal through contrast, tactility, quiet typography and clear order of discovery.

The phrase “engineered to make the customer cry” is rejected. The ethical objective is recognition, delight, dignity and ease of sharing—not emotional coercion.

## 7. Privacy trust as a product feature

Personalisation creates a privacy paradox: the more intimate the input, the more valuable the output may feel, but the perceived risk rises too. Research on trust in personalisation services discusses the importance of transparency and data handling; it does not justify claiming that privacy controls automatically increase conversion. [Personalisation and trust research overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S0736376124000107).

Turn privacy into a tangible promise only after it is proven:

1. Collect only information needed for the order.
2. Separate fulfilment consent from marketing consent.
3. Identify each processor and disclose the relevant data path.
4. Do not generate or retain child face embeddings or LoRA weights.
5. Do not use any customer's child likeness in ads, social posts, demo pages or public portfolio.
6. Delete raw uploads and derivatives under a tested retention process, and provide a truthful deletion receipt.
7. Give the customer an understandable export and deletion-request path.
8. Do not claim “zero retention” unless the exact provider contract and account configuration substantiate it.

Suggested public copy (only after proof): “Your family’s reference images are used only for the commissioned production workflow. We do not use a child’s likeness in public marketing. Our deletion process is documented and verified across the systems under our control.”

## 8. Five buyer personas and conversion copy

These are testable personas, not measured segments. Validate them through opt-in interviews and paid-pilot conversion data.

### Persona A — The careful parent
- **Job to be done:** a beautiful shared-reading gift with safe, accurate, age-appropriate content.
- **Emotional drivers:** care, recognition, shared ritual.
- **Objections:** “Will the face look strange?”, “Who sees the photo?”, “Can I approve the pages?”, “Is this actually a quality hardcover?”
- **Proof required:** sample pages, exact paper and binding spec, clear photo policy, parent proof, realistic dispatch estimate.
- **Conversion message:** “A story shaped around your child’s interests—not a promise about who they should become. Review the pages before anything is printed, and see exactly how reference images are handled.”

### Persona B — The grandparent gifting a keepsake
- **Job to be done:** give a memorable, personal gift to a grandchild or family member.
- **Drivers:** connection, family ritual, a physical object to revisit.
- **Objections:** digital complexity, uncertain delivery, unfamiliar AI production.
- **Proof required:** a simple assisted process, printed sample, readable gift note and gift delivery clarity.
- **Conversion message:** “A story made for one family moment, with names and details checked before it becomes a book you can read together.”

### Persona C — The adult preserving family history
- **Job to be done:** organise memories, values and family stories into a durable book.
- **Drivers:** generativity, continuity, preserving language and family knowledge.
- **Objections:** “Will the writing sound generic?”, “Will facts be invented?”, “Who owns the material?”, “Can I export it?”
- **Proof required:** interview/question framework, source ledger, editorial revision scope, export and licence terms.
- **Conversion message:** “Your memories remain the source. We organise, edit and design them into a readable family edition, with factual details checked against the material you approve.”

### Persona D — The luxury hotel guest-experience director
- **Job to be done:** pilot a distinctive welcome, family or loyalty gift without creating service failures.
- **Drivers:** guest recognition, brand consistency, operational control.
- **Objections:** procurement, turnaround, data protection, cost per guest, supplier reliability.
- **Proof required:** a paid five-book pilot, SKU and quote, neutral synthetic sample, data-processing map, operational KPIs.
- **Conversion message:** “Start with five commissioned guest editions. We will measure approval rate, delivery integrity and guest feedback before proposing a wider programme.”

### Persona E — The international-school enrichment lead
- **Job to be done:** create a curriculum-aligned reading or yearbook-style project without collecting unnecessary student data.
- **Drivers:** literacy, cultural representation, school community.
- **Objections:** safeguarding, consent, curriculum fit, procurement and workload.
- **Proof required:** synthetic demo, school-controlled consent, no direct child accounts in the first pilot, curriculum mapping, accessibility review.
- **Conversion message:** “A school-approved story project built around your learning objective. The first pilot uses synthetic characters; any student-personalised edition is a separate, safeguarding-reviewed scope.”

## 9. Measurement plan

| Hypothesis | Metric | Minimum evidence before acting |
|---|---|---|
| A physical sample increases confidence | Qualified sample-to-order conversion | Compare matched buyer conversations; do not infer from compliments |
| Privacy explanation reduces objection frequency | Share of qualified enquiries raising photo concerns | Record objections with consent; never store unnecessary child data |
| Name proof reduces costly errors | Proper-noun correction rate before print and post-delivery | Track corrections per approved book |
| A choice of story tone increases relevance | Selection distribution and satisfaction | Ask buyers; avoid leading surveys |
| Unboxing is share-worthy | Opt-in customer-generated share rate | Ask permission; never assume permission from order completion |

| Decision | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
